Introduction — The SSP as CMMC Foundation
📌
The System Security Plan is the single most important document in a CMMC assessment. It serves as the authoritative blueprint of the organization's cybersecurity posture — describing what systems exist, how CUI is protected, who is responsible, and which controls are implemented. An incomplete or inaccurate SSP signals to a C3PAO assessor that the organization does not fully understand its own environment.
How Assessors Use the SSP
  • First document reviewed — sets scope for all subsequent evidence requests
  • Defines what systems are in-scope for the assessment
  • Identifies named responsible parties the assessor will interview
  • Describes which controls are implemented, planned, or inherited
  • Maps to each of the 110 NIST SP 800-171 practices the C3PAO must evaluate
  • Provides the baseline against which POA&M entries are validated
Consequences of an Incomplete SSP
  • Assessors cannot validate controls they cannot locate in the document
  • Missing systems may surface during interviews — expanding scope unexpectedly
  • No named owner = assessor cannot confirm accountability during interviews
  • Boundary gaps suggest the contractor doesn't know where CUI lives
  • Control gaps that should be in POA&M become unresolved findings
  • SPRS score submission may be challenged if SSP doesn't support it
🎯
Learning Objectives
1
Explain the purpose, structure, and importance of the System Security Plan in the CMMC Level 2 assessment process and its relationship to NIST SP 800-171.
2
Identify the major sections of a CMMC SSP and determine what organizational information is required to complete each section accurately.
3
Define the FFSI assessment boundary by categorizing systems as in-scope, shared, external, or out-of-scope — and justify each determination based on CUI flow and connectivity.
4
Evaluate FFSI's current cybersecurity posture by identifying implemented controls, known gaps, security tool coverage, and personnel assignments across all facilities.
5
Complete missing SSP worksheet fields using FFSI case study data — including system descriptions, authentication methods, encryption strategies, and incident response capabilities.
6
Assign CMMC SSP ownership roles — System Owner, ISSO, Data Owner, Compliance Manager, and others — with justifications based on actual FFSI personnel responsibilities.
7
Identify SSP deficiencies and develop a preliminary Plan of Action and Milestones (POA&M) with prioritized remediation actions and responsible owners.
8
Prepare a concise executive readiness briefing summarizing SSP weaknesses, missing documentation, and recommended priorities for the 12-month path to C3PAO assessment.
