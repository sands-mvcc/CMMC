About This Case Study

ThreadBridge Solutions, LLC is a fictional small business that manufactures both combat utility uniforms for the Department of Defense and sells commercial office supplies to federal agencies via GSA Schedule. This dual-revenue model places ThreadBridge at the intersection of two distinct compliance regimes: CMMC Level 2 (for CUI-laden defense contracts) and CMMC Level 1 (for commercial GSA supply). Learners will analyze the company's technical environment, identify security gaps, evaluate two real security incidents, and produce the eleven practitioner documents required for a CMMC Level 2 assessment. The scenario deliberately embeds multiple realistic compliance failures common in small-to-midsize defense contractors.

Industry Context
Defense Textile & Apparel — DoD Uniform Manufacturing Sector

The defense uniform manufacturing sector is a specialized niche of the broader defense industrial base (DIB). Companies operating in this space typically hold Defense Logistics Agency (DLA) contracts for combat utility uniforms, physical fitness uniforms, and service dress components. The sector is characterized by high volume, tight delivery schedules, strict technical specifications, and substantial CUI obligations—particularly around material composition and camouflage pattern data that DLA provides to contractors. ThreadBridge's Unique Position: Unlike pure-play defense apparel manufacturers or pure commercial suppliers, ThreadBridge occupies the intersection of both product lines—manufacturing combat utility uniforms under a DLA IDIQ (triggering CMMC Level 2 obligations) while simultaneously supplying office materials via GSA Schedule (Level 1 only). This dual exposure creates compliance complexity: different data classification requirements, different flow-down clauses, and different assessment timelines must be managed simultaneously within a single 82-person organization.

G-01 CRITICAL IA MFA only on M365 — ERP, File Server, DC RDP, VPN, NAS all unprotected ▼
IA.L2-3.5.3
Multi-factor authentication is enforced only on Microsoft 365 via Conditional Access. The ERP server (Epicor 10.2), file server, domain controller RDP, SSL VPN, and backup NAS all rely on single-factor AD credentials or local passwords. This means a compromised password—through phishing, credential stuffing, or insider threat—provides direct access to CUI with no secondary verification.
CRITICAL — all CUI systems accessible with a single credential. Combined with INC-001 (phishing credential exposure), this represents an active attack surface.
G-02 CRITICAL AC No Access Control Matrix; Dennis manages access from memory; orphaned QB admin account (14 months) ▼
AC.L2-3.1.1, AC.L2-3.1.2
No formal access control matrix exists. Dennis Park manages user permissions from memory with no documented baseline. A QuickBooks administrator account has been active for 14 months after its owner left the company. There is no account review or recertification process. Sandra Ybarra is unsure who has access to the CUI contract folder she requested be restricted months ago.
CRITICAL — orphaned privileged accounts are a top initial access vector. Without an access matrix, separation of duties and least privilege cannot be enforced or audited.
G-03 HIGH AU Logs enabled but never reviewed; no retention policy; no SIEM ▼
AU.L2-3.3.1, AU.L2-3.3.2
Windows Event Logs and Sophos firewall logs are enabled but Dennis Park only reviews them reactively (when a problem arises). No retention schedule is documented. No SIEM or log aggregation tool is in use. The 30-day camera retention exists but is not linked to an overall audit/review program.
HIGH — without regular log review, intrusions (e.g., TechServ after-hours access, lateral movement post-phishing) would go undetected. Retention gaps may also violate contractual obligations.
G-04 CRITICAL RA Last vuln scan 14 months ago; no recurring schedule; no remediation tracking ▼
RA.L2-3.11.2
A third-party vulnerability scan was conducted approximately 14 months ago. Dennis Park received only a summary (not a full report). Some findings were patched; remediation was not tracked. No recurring scan schedule exists. Several systems (ERP, NAS, workstations) have not been formally assessed in over a year.
CRITICAL — unpatched vulnerabilities on CUI-bearing systems create direct exploitation risk. Without tracking, there is no way to confirm prior findings were fully remediated.
G-05 CRITICAL IR No written IRP; 2 incidents handled informally by email; no lessons learned ▼
IR.L2-3.6.1, IR.L2-3.6.2
No written Incident Response Plan exists. INC-001 (phishing/credential exposure) and INC-002 (lost laptop) were both handled via informal email chains with no formal investigation, timeline, or root cause analysis. Neither incident was evaluated against DFARS 252.204-7012 reporting requirements. Sandra Ybarra made a judgment call not to report INC-001 without consulting legal counsel or the contracting officer.
CRITICAL — DFARS reporting failures can constitute False Claims Act violations. Absence of an IRP means the next incident will also be mishandled.
G-06 HIGH CM No baseline configs; no change management process; Dennis installs software on request ▼
CM.L2-3.4.1, CM.L2-3.4.3
No documented baseline configurations exist for any system. Dennis Park installs requested software without a formal approval process, security review, or change log. There is no authorized software list. Software on the 40 workstations is inconsistent and undocumented.
HIGH — uncontrolled software introduces malware risk and creates an inconsistent security posture that cannot be audited or reproduced.
G-07 CRITICAL MA TechServ has ERP access (CUI) with no CUI clause, no escort requirement, no media sanitization ▼
MA.L2-3.7.5
TechServ LLC has remote VPN access and on-site access to the ERP server, which contains CUI. Their contract (basic MSA) contains no CUI handling clause, no security requirements, and no flow-down obligations. Dennis Park monitors their on-site work informally but cannot detect or control after-hours remote access. No media sanitization requirement exists for any equipment they handle.
CRITICAL — third-party access to CUI without appropriate contractual protections is a direct DFARS flow-down violation and potential data exposure risk.
G-08 CRITICAL PL No SSP exists — foundational CMMC document entirely absent ▼
PL.L2-3.12.1
ThreadBridge has no System Security Plan. Dennis Park has never documented the system boundary, data flows, control implementations, or interconnections. The SSP is the foundational document for CMMC assessment — without it, no assessment can be completed, no POA&M can be validated, and the organization cannot demonstrate systematic NIST 800-171 compliance.
CRITICAL — an SSP is required by DFARS 252.204-7012 and is the primary artifact reviewed by a C3PAO during a CMMC Level 2 assessment. Its absence alone could result in contract suspension.
G-09 HIGH SC No VLANs; unmanaged Android tablets on same LAN as ERP server and file server ▼
SC.L2-3.13.3
The Cisco SG350 switches have no VLAN configuration. The manufacturing floor Android tablets (Android 12, not MDM-enrolled) are on the same flat network as the ERP server and file server containing CUI. An Android device could be used to access ERP via web browser with only a username/password.
HIGH — network segmentation is a foundational security control. Flat networks dramatically expand the blast radius of any compromise.
G-10 HIGH MP No USB/removable media policy; no sanitization procedure; USB drives used freely ▼
MP.L2-3.8.7
No policy governs use of USB drives or removable media. Employees freely use personal USB drives on workstations, including those that handle CUI. No media sanitization procedure exists for retiring or repurposing drives. No DLP controls are in place to block unauthorized file transfers.
HIGH — uncontrolled removable media is a major data exfiltration and malware introduction vector.
G-11 MEDIUM AT 60% training completion; no role-based training for IT/CUI handlers ▼
AT.L2-3.2.2
KnowBe4 phishing and security awareness training is provided annually but only 49/82 employees (60%) have completed it. The training content does not specifically address CUI handling requirements, DFARS obligations, or the company's specific data classification policies. No role-based supplemental training exists for CUI handlers or IT staff.
MEDIUM — with 40% of staff untrained, the next phishing incident (see INC-001) is likely. Without CUI-specific training, employees cannot recognize when they are handling regulated data.
G-12 MEDIUM PE No visitor access log for server room; no badge access at Frostburg ▼
PE.L1-3.10.3
While the Cumberland server room has keypad access, there is no visitor log for third-party technicians (Ricoh, TechServ) who access the space. The Frostburg warehouse facility has no electronic badge access — physical entry is unmonitored and unlogged. Ten workstations at Frostburg handle FCI.
MEDIUM — unlogged physical access prevents forensic reconstruction of physical security events and creates accountability gaps.
G-13 CRITICAL RA/SPRS SPRS score never submitted — DFARS 252.204-7019 requires current score on record ▼
DFARS 252.204-7019
ThreadBridge has never calculated or submitted a score to the Supplier Performance Risk System (SPRS). DFARS 252.204-7019 requires contractors to complete a NIST SP 800-171 self-assessment and post the score to SPRS before contract award and annually thereafter. The DLA contract FA4890-25-D-0014 requires this. The current situation means ThreadBridge is operating in violation of its active contract terms.
CRITICAL — SPRS non-submission is a contractual violation and can trigger a stop-work order, contract termination, or referral to the DoD IG. With a CMMC assessment renewal 18 months out, this must be remediated immediately.

Should INC-001 have triggered DFARS 252.204-7012 notification?

Correct. The compromised M365 account had access to SharePoint, which contains CUI (uniform specs, delivery destinations). The 72-hour reporting clock under DFARS 252.204-7012 began when ThreadBridge learned of the phishing incident. Failure to report where CUI was potentially accessible constitutes a reporting violation and creates potential False Claims Act exposure.
Incorrect. Even though Ybarra concluded "nothing was stolen," the compromised M365 account had access to SharePoint folders containing CUI. The reporting obligation under DFARS 252.204-7012 triggers when a cyber incident affects a system that processes, stores, or transmits CUI — not only when data is confirmed stolen. A forensic determination was never made.

INC-002 — Lost/Stolen Device

~4 months ago | Severity: MEDIUM
Jordan Reyes (remote sales rep)
Reported work laptop missing after a business trip; laptop was BitLocker-encrypted
AD account disabled; M365 remote wipe via Intune; VPN access revoked
Single email from Reyes to Dennis Park — no formal record, no CUI exposure determination
Partial — no determination of CUI accessibility made
NO — no formal determination made either way

Should INC-002 have triggered DFARS 252.204-7012 notification?

Possibly correct. Whether reporting was required depends on whether the laptop contained or could access CUI. Jordan Reyes is in sales — primarily GSA/FCI work. If CUI was never on the device or accessible from it, no reporting obligation may exist. However, the key failure is that a formal determination was never made. Best practice requires documenting the determination either way.
Possibly correct, but the process was wrong. BitLocker encryption mitigates the risk of data exposure from a lost laptop. If the device never contained or accessed CUI, the reporting obligation may not apply. However, this determination was never formally documented. The correct approach is: assess the device's data exposure, document the conclusion, and then make an informed reporting decision — not simply assume no reporting is needed.

Incident Analysis — Key Takeaways

  • DFARS 252.204-7012 requires reporting cyber incidents on systems that process, store, or transmit CUI — the threshold is system access, not confirmed data theft.
  • The 72-hour reporting clock begins when the contractor discovers the incident, not when they confirm a breach.
  • Both incidents lacked a documented root cause analysis, leaving the organization vulnerable to recurrence.
  • INC-001 combined with G-01 (no MFA on ERP) represents an unresolved attack chain that remains active.
  • Lesson: Every potential incident should produce a written record — even if the conclusion is "no reporting required, here's why."
