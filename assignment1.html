🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this activity, participants will be able to:

📖 Key Terms
🏭 Scenario

Federal Food Services Inc. (FFSI) manufactures Meals Ready-to-Eat (MREs) and supplies food products to the U.S. Army under six active federal contracts totaling over $234 million. The company is preparing for a CMMC Level 2 assessment.

FFSI stores and processes the following types of information:

  • Federal contract information (FCI) and Army delivery schedules
  • Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI) — packaging specs, unit pricing, logistics node data
  • Supplier and subcontractor records
  • Employee personnel information
  • Production specifications and quality assurance reports
  • Shipping and logistics tracking data

Your consulting team has been asked to review the FFSI personnel roster and assign appropriate data governance roles to ensure CUI is properly owned, protected, processed, governed, and controlled.

🗣 Instructor Debrief (5 Minutes)

Ask participants:

  • Which role was easiest to assign? Which created the most debate?
  • How could incorrect role assignments create CMMC findings?
  • What evidence would an assessor request to verify these assignments?

Expected Takeaway: Successful CMMC implementation requires clear accountability for CUI. Organizations must be able to identify who owns data, who protects it, who uses it, who governs it, and whose information is being stored and processed. This accountability is a key component of effective cybersecurity governance and CMMC readiness.
