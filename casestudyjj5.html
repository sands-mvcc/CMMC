Organizational Overview

Coastal Defense Consulting Group (CDCG) is a small DoD management consulting firm based in McLean, Virginia, providing acquisition support, program management, and policy advisory services to OSD and military departments.

🏢 Company Profile

Coastal Defense Consulting Group (CDCG) is a knowledge-work defense contractor whose primary business is supporting government clients through acquisition analysis, program management support, executive briefings, policy documentation, and advisory services. Unlike a manufacturing contractor, CDCG’s CUI environment is centered on documents, email, SharePoint, Teams, and consultant laptops rather than production systems or industrial equipment.

🌐 Operating Environment

CDCG operates from McLean, Virginia and supports hybrid work across headquarters, client locations, and home offices. Staff regularly access contract data remotely, collaborate through Microsoft 365, and exchange working documents with government stakeholders and selected subcontractors.

🔐 CUI Environment

Most Controlled Unclassified Information in this case resides in draft reports, acquisition documents, policy memos, schedules, briefing materials, email, and SharePoint/Teams collaboration spaces. This makes secure document handling, identity management, remote access, and collaboration controls especially important.

⚠️ Key Scenario Risks

  • • Home use is not consistently protected by enforced VPN access.
  • • A legacy document management system is not clearly included in SSP scope.
  • • High staff turnover contributes to stale access and delayed offboarding.
  • • Some security and compliance weaknesses are known but not fully remediated.

Security Posture

CDCG has a partially developed CMMC Level 2 security program with several important controls in place, but multiple known weaknesses remain in CUI handling, remote access, logging, system scoping, and personnel-related access management.

📊 Overall Posture Summary

CDCG has established the basic structure of a CMMC-aligned security program, including policies, role definitions, training expectations, cloud collaboration controls, and some incident handling processes. However, the environment still shows important inconsistencies between documented expectations and actual day-to-day practice.

The company’s security posture is strongest where controls are centralized, such as in baseline policy, account administration, and standard cloud collaboration tools. The posture is weaker where secure behavior depends on consistent user action, complete scoping, timely offboarding, and stronger enforcement of remote-access and CUI-handling requirements.

✅ Areas of Relative Strength

  • • CDCG has a defined target of CMMC Level 2 and has already identified known gap areas.
  • • Core collaboration workflows are centered on a manageable set of platforms.
  • • Company-managed laptops and centralized cloud services provide a reasonable foundation.
  • • Security policies, training expectations, and incident response processes exist.
  • • Leadership awareness of the need to improve compliance readiness.

❌ Known Gap Areas

  • • CUI transmitted through standard email instead of protected methods.
  • • Remote access is not always restricted through approved VPN workflows.
  • • Legacy DMS excluded from documented SSP boundary.
  • • Logging and monitoring inconsistently applied across systems.
  • • High turnover and delayed offboarding creating stale account risks.
  • • Inconsistent enforcement of existing policy expectations.

🔎 Readiness Observations

CDCG is not starting from zero; the company already has enough structure in place for students to see a recognizable compliance program. At the same time, the case is intentionally built so students can identify the difference between having documented controls and having consistently implemented controls.

Students should come away understanding that CDCG’s readiness problem is not simply the absence of cybersecurity activity. The larger issue is that several important control areas are only partially implemented, incompletely scoped, weakly enforced, or not translated into sustained operational practice.

This tab should help students distinguish between documented security intent and real operational effectiveness across the case environment.

Policies & Procedures

These sample internal policies show how CDCG expects personnel to handle CUI, use remote access, and remove access when staff leave or change roles. Students should compare these written expectations to how the organization actually operates in the case.

Purpose and Scope

This standard defines how CDCG identifies, marks, stores, transmits, and disposes of CUI across email, SharePoint, Teams, laptops, and approved internal systems. It applies to all employees, contractors, and systems that handle contract-related CUI.

Requirements

  • • CUI must be identified based on customer markings, contract context, and internal review.
  • • Documents and emails containing CUI must be clearly marked.
  • • CUI must only be stored in approved company systems and approved collaboration locations.
  • • CUI must not be stored in personal accounts, non-approved platforms, or general-purpose shared areas.

Response to Improper Handling

  • • If CUI is found in the wrong location, users must notify IT or security immediately.
  • • The incident must be documented and the information relocated, removed, or otherwise remediated.

Purpose and Scope

This standard governs remote access to CDCG systems from home offices, travel locations, and other non-corporate work sites. It applies to all users who connect to company resources from outside formal office locations.

Requirements

  • • Personnel handling CUI must use approved company devices and approved remote-access methods.
  • • Remote sessions involving CUI must be protected through managed access controls and secure communications.
  • • Personal devices may not be used to store or download CUI.
  • • Users must follow secure home-workspace expectations when handling sensitive information remotely.

Prohibited Practices

  • • Sending CUI from personal email accounts
  • • Downloading CUI to unmanaged devices
  • • Using non-approved file-sharing tools for contract work

Purpose and Scope

This standard establishes the minimum process for disabling, removing, or updating user access when employees, subcontractors, or temporary personnel leave CDCG or move to new assignments.

Requirements

  • • HR and management must notify IT of departures and role changes as soon as they are known.
  • • Access to email, SharePoint, Teams, VPN, file shares, and relevant systems must be removed promptly.
  • • Company devices, tokens, and related access materials must be collected or disabled.
  • • Temporary exceptions must be documented, approved, and time-limited.

Access Review Expectation

  • • Role changes should trigger review of prior permissions and project-related access.
  • • Users should retain only the access needed for current responsibilities.

Instructor Guide

Coastal Defense Consulting Group (CDCG) – CMMC Level 2 Audit Training Facilitation Guide

This guide supports instructors using the Coastal Defense Consulting Group case study in CMMC Level 2 audit training. It explains the instructional purpose of the case, highlights the major seeded issues, and helps facilitators connect case evidence to the formal audit artifacts students are expected to produce.

Instructional Purpose

The CDCG case is designed to help students analyze a small defense consulting firm whose CUI environment is centered on documents, collaboration tools, remote work, and user-managed workflows rather than laboratories, manufacturing systems, or engineering platforms. The case emphasizes how CMMC concerns can arise in ordinary business systems when CUI is created, shared, stored, or accessed through email, Teams, SharePoint, laptops, and legacy repositories.

This case is especially useful for helping students understand that compliance problems often emerge from incomplete scoping, inconsistent control enforcement, user behavior, weak follow-through, and poorly coordinated access management rather than from the total absence of cybersecurity structure.

Scenario Summary

  • Organization: Coastal Defense Consulting Group (CDCG)
  • Type: Small DoD management consulting contractor
  • Location: McLean, Virginia
  • Employees: 38
  • Revenue: ~$9M
  • Active Contracts: 3
  • Target Level: CMMC Level 2

The company supports acquisition, program management, and policy advisory work. Findings primarily involve identity, access, and secure collaboration.

Core Seeded Weaknesses

  • • Unencrypted or weakly controlled email workflows.
  • • Inconsistent VPN-only methods for remote access.
  • • Legacy DMS containing CUI excluded from SSP scope.
  • • Delayed offboarding creating stale-account risks.
  • • Imbalanced logging maturity across systems.
  • • Operational practice divergence from written policy.

Learning Objectives

  • Identify CUI boundaries in cloud-heavy environments
  • Distinguish policy from actual implementation
  • Recognize remote work effects on CUI protection
  • Evaluate offboarding as a control problem
  • Translate evidence into formal audit artifacts
  • Explain readiness impacts of repeat incidents

How to Frame the Case for Students

Instructors should emphasize that CDCG is not a “broken” company with no cybersecurity structure. Instead, it is a realistic small contractor that has policies, tools, and awareness of compliance expectations, but still operates with important gaps between formal security intent and operational consistency.

Student work should be evaluated less on whether they use identical wording and more on whether they demonstrate defensible scoping, evidence-based reasoning, consistent treatment of repeated issues, and appropriate placement of findings across the audit artifacts. Strong submissions should show cross-tab thinking and should distinguish one-time events from structural weaknesses.
