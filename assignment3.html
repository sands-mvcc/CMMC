Introduction — Why Asset Inventories Matter
🎯
An accurate, well-categorized asset inventory is one of the first documents a C3PAO assessor will request. It defines the assessment boundary, drives resource allocation, and reveals whether the contractor truly understands where CUI lives in their environment. Incorrect categorization — in either direction — creates real consequences: assets missed from scope create compliance risk; assets over-scoped inflate assessment cost and effort.
Why Assessors Review Inventories First
  • Validates that the contractor knows where CUI enters, lives, and exits
  • Confirms the SSP boundary matches what's actually deployed
  • Identifies unmanaged or unclaimed assets before interviews begin
  • Reveals segmentation gaps that expand scope unexpectedly
  • Sets the foundation for all 110 NIST SP 800-171 practice assessments
Consequences of Improper Categorization
  • Under-scoping: CUI assets excluded → compliance gaps → assessment failure
  • Over-scoping: Non-CUI assets included → higher cost, more practices to evidence
  • Missing OT assets: Production systems connected to CUI VLANs go unreviewed
  • Unowned assets: No owner = no evidence = assessor finding
  • Stale inventory: Assets added post-SSP create undocumented scope gaps

Learning Objectives
1
Identify and apply the five CMMC asset categories — CUI Assets, Security Protection Assets, Contractor Risk Managed Assets, Specialized Assets, and Out-of-Scope Assets — to a real-world asset list.
2
Determine whether an asset processes, stores, or transmits CUI and explain how that determination affects its assessment scope and required security controls.
3
Distinguish between in-scope, indirectly in-scope, and out-of-scope assets within a contractor's environment and justify each determination with reference to CUI flow and segmentation.
4
Complete missing fields within a sample asset inventory worksheet — including asset owner, category, CUI status, criticality, and security controls — using information from the FFSI case study.
5
Assign Asset Owner, Asset Custodian, and Technical Administrator roles to selected FFSI assets and explain the accountability difference between each role.
6
Identify common inventory weaknesses in the FFSI environment and develop three concrete recommendations for improving the organization's asset management posture prior to assessment.
7
Explain how segmentation, enclave design, and access control boundaries affect which assets fall within the CMMC assessment scope.
