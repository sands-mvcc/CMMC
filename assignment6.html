Introduction — The CMMC Applicability Study
🎯
A CMMC Applicability Study is the foundational analysis every defense contractor should perform before investing in compliance activities. It answers the most basic — and most important — question: Does CMMC apply to us, and if so, why, where, and at what level? Getting this wrong wastes resources, creates blind spots, and can result in contract loss or failed assessments.
Why Applicability Studies Matter
  • Not all DoD contracts trigger CMMC — FAR 52.204-21 vs. DFARS 252.204-7012 have different requirements
  • CUI handling — not just contract type — determines whether Level 1 or Level 2 applies
  • Scope decisions made here drive all downstream activities: SSP, POA&M, asset inventory, training
  • Over-scoping wastes resources; under-scoping creates compliance risk and assessment failure
  • Applicability studies establish the legal and contractual basis for all compliance decisions
What Drives CMMC Obligations
  • Contract language — DFARS clauses 252.204-7012, -7019, -7020, -7021 trigger specific obligations
  • Data classification — CUI presence requires Level 2; FCI-only may require only Level 1
  • Subcontract flowdown — prime contractor clauses flow to subs who handle covered information
  • System connectivity — systems connected to CUI assets expand the assessment boundary
  • Personnel roles — staff who access, handle, or protect CUI are subject to CMMC requirements
🎯
Learning Objectives
1
Explain the purpose of a CMMC Applicability Study and why it is the first — and most important — step in a contractor's CMMC compliance journey.
2
Analyze FFSI's federal contracts to identify which DFARS clauses are present, which create CMMC obligations, and which specific requirements each clause triggers.
3
Classify organizational data as FCI, CUI, or neither — justifying each determination using NIST SP 800-171 and CUI Registry definitions.
4
Determine which FFSI facilities, departments, systems, cloud services, and vendors fall within the CMMC assessment scope and justify inclusions and exclusions.
5
Identify the CMMC assessment boundary — distinguishing between included assets, shared assets, supporting assets, and excluded assets.
6
Evaluate FFSI's personnel roster and determine which roles are subject to CMMC requirements, training obligations, and CUI handling responsibilities.
7
Identify documentation FFSI must develop, update, or create as part of achieving CMMC Level 2 certification readiness.
8
Synthesize applicability findings into a concise executive recommendation that answers whether CMMC applies, why, at what level, and what FFSI should do first.
