Executive Introduction
🎯
Why this exercise matters: Most mid-sized DIB contractors cannot achieve CMMC Level 2 certification using only internal resources. They must strategically combine internal staffing, certified professionals, training programs, managed service providers, and third-party assessors — the CMMC Ecosystem. Making the wrong choices wastes budget, delays certification, and creates compliance gaps that can cost contracts.
Why Outside Assistance Is Usually Required
  • CMMC Level 2 requires 110 NIST SP 800-171 practices across 14 domains
  • Most contractors lack in-house CMMC expertise at the time of assessment
  • C3PAO assessors must be independent — contractors cannot self-assess at Level 2
  • Gaps in SSPs, POA&Ms, and policies require specialized documentation skills
  • Ongoing managed security services support continuous compliance between assessments
Why Internal Staffing & Certifications Matter
  • Assessors will interview internal staff — they must be knowledgeable and prepared
  • Certified CMMC Professionals (CCPs) provide credible internal compliance leadership
  • Registered Practitioners (RPs) can guide preparation without conducting assessments
  • Internal ownership of the SSP and POA&M is required by DFARS 252.204-7020
  • Long-term compliance requires embedded staff, not just periodic consultants
🎯
Learning Objectives
1
Identify the roles and functions of key CMMC Ecosystem entities — including OSA, RP, RPO, CCP, CCA, C3PAO, MSP, and MSSP — and explain how each supports a contractor's path to certification.
2
Analyze an organization's existing personnel structure and determine appropriate CMMC role assignments, certification paths, and training priorities for key staff members.
3
Evaluate a set of cybersecurity and compliance readiness challenges and develop a realistic workforce plan — including decisions to hire, train, certify, or outsource each capability.
4
Design a training and certification roadmap with prioritized timelines (0–3, 3–6, and 6–12 months) aligned to assessment readiness milestones.
5
Assess a set of CMMC-required documents and determine which exist, which need updates, and which must be created — prioritizing based on assessment impact.
6
Distinguish between capabilities that should remain internal vs. those that should be outsourced, justifying each decision in terms of cost, risk, expertise, and long-term compliance sustainability.
7
Synthesize findings into a concise executive briefing that addresses staffing gaps, certification priorities, ecosystem partner selection, and documentation priorities.
